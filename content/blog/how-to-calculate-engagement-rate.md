Calculate engagement rate by adding a post's likes, comments, and shares, dividing the total by the account's followers, then multiplying by 100. Compare the result with platform-specific ranges—not a universal score—because audience size, content format, and measurement methods affect what counts as good engagement.

## How do you calculate engagement rate?

For a follower-based, per-post rate, use this formula:

**Engagement rate (%) = (Likes + Comments + Shares) / Followers × 100**

For example, if a post receives 120 likes, 18 comments, and 12 shares from an account with 5,000 followers, add the interactions (150), divide by 5,000, and multiply by 100. The engagement rate is **3%**.

Use the same type of interaction counts and follower denominator when comparing posts. This calculation measures the listed interactions relative to followers; it is not the only method. Other reports may calculate engagement against reach, impressions, or video views, so check the method before comparing figures.

## What is a good engagement rate on Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube?

As broad reference ranges, Convaudit's calculator uses **1–3% for Instagram**, **3–6% for TikTok**, and **2–5% for YouTube**. These are comparison points, not official platform standards or guarantees. Results vary with audience size, content type, posting frequency, and the specific interactions included in the calculation.

A rate below a range does not by itself mean a post failed, and a rate above it does not guarantee business results. Compare similar content on the same platform over time, and consider reach and audience relevance alongside the percentage. Avoid treating different platforms' rates as directly equivalent.

## When should you use a different engagement formula?

Use a reach-based or impression-based calculation when those are the metrics available or relevant to your goal, such as evaluating how people who actually saw a post responded. Divide the selected interactions by reach or impressions, then multiply by 100. Do not compare that result directly with a follower-based rate without labeling the difference.

For video platforms, an engagement rate based on views can answer a different question from one based on subscribers. Choose the denominator that matches the analysis, use it consistently, and state what interactions were counted. Clear definitions make reports more useful than a single number without context.

## What are common questions about calculating engagement rate?

### What is the formula for engagement rate?

For a follower-based post rate, add likes, comments, and shares, divide their sum by the account's follower count, and multiply by 100. Written as a formula: **(Likes + Comments + Shares) / Followers × 100**. Other methods use reach, impressions, or views as the denominator, so identify the formula when reporting or comparing results.

### Is a 3% engagement rate good?

A 3% rate falls within the broad Instagram and YouTube reference ranges used by Convaudit's calculator, and below its TikTok reference range. That does not make it universally good or bad: audience size, content format, the denominator, and which interactions you counted all matter. Compare similar posts on the same platform for more useful context.

### Are TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube benchmarks the same?

No. The platforms differ in content formats, audience behavior, and measurement conventions. Convaudit uses broad reference ranges of 1–3% for Instagram, 3–6% for TikTok, and 2–5% for YouTube. Treat them as starting points rather than official standards, and compare like-for-like posts using the same denominator and interaction types.

### Should I calculate engagement by followers or by reach?

Choose the denominator based on what you want to understand. Followers can help compare post interactions with an account's audience size, while reach measures interactions relative to people who saw the content. The resulting percentages answer different questions. Label your method, use it consistently, and avoid comparing a reach-based result with a follower-based benchmark.
