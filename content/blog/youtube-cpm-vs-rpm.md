YouTube CPM and RPM measure different parts of video monetization. CPM describes advertiser cost per 1,000 ad impressions (or monetized playbacks for playback-based CPM); RPM estimates creator revenue per 1,000 video views. Since the metrics use different amounts and denominators, CPM is not the same as the money a creator earns for every thousand views.

## What does CPM mean on YouTube?

CPM stands for cost per mille, or cost per 1,000. In YouTube Analytics, CPM is the amount advertisers pay for 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube's revenue share is applied. An ad impression is counted when an ad is shown; one video view does not necessarily mean one ad impression.

You may also see **playback-based CPM**, which measures advertiser cost per 1,000 monetized playbacks. A playback is considered monetized when a viewer watches a video and is shown at least one ad. This metric uses a different denominator from impression-based CPM, so check which label you are comparing.

## What does RPM mean on YouTube?

RPM means revenue per mille. It estimates the creator's revenue per 1,000 video views after YouTube's share, based on the revenue included in the metric. Depending on the Analytics report, that revenue can include ads and other YouTube monetization sources, while the view count can include views that did not show an ad.

RPM is useful for understanding how much revenue your channel generated relative to its views during a period. It is not an advertiser's bid, a fixed rate for future views, or a guarantee that another video will earn the same amount.

## How do you calculate CPM and RPM?

The basic calculations use different inputs:

- **CPM** = advertiser cost ÷ ad impressions × 1,000
- **RPM** = creator revenue ÷ total video views × 1,000

For example, if advertisers spent $1,200 for 100,000 ad impressions, the impression-based CPM is $12. If a channel generated $600 in included revenue from 100,000 video views, its RPM for that period is $6. These examples use different totals; they do not imply that a $12 CPM produces a $6 RPM.

You cannot reliably calculate your channel's RPM by multiplying CPM by a fixed share. Not every view has an ad impression, and RPM can include revenue sources beyond ads. Revenue share and eligibility can also differ by monetization product. ## Why do YouTube CPM and RPM change?

Both figures can vary between videos and over time. Common factors include:

- **Viewer location:** advertisers may bid differently across regions and audiences.
- **Time of year:** advertising demand can rise or fall by season.
- **Topic and audience:** some subjects attract different advertiser demand.
- **Ad availability and format:** not every view is monetized, and ad formats have different economics.
- **Video and channel eligibility:** content suitability, audience settings, and monetization status can affect available revenue.
- **Revenue mix:** memberships, Premium, and other eligible sources can affect RPM without changing ad-impression CPM in the same way.

Use the values in YouTube Studio for the period you are analyzing, and compare like with like. A channel-wide monthly RPM and a single video's playback-based CPM answer different questions.

## When should you look at CPM or RPM?

Use CPM to understand advertiser demand and the price of ad inventory. Use RPM to compare creator revenue against views, especially when looking at changes in performance over time. Neither metric alone explains why income changed: compare the date range, view count, monetized playbacks, audience, and revenue sources in Analytics.

If you are forecasting, model a range rather than assuming a single rate. The [YouTube Money Calculator](/tools/youtube-money-calculator) can help explore how broad niche CPM estimates affect potential ad revenue, but the result is not a payout quote. Your actual Analytics data and current YouTube monetization terms are more relevant to your channel.

## What are common questions about YouTube CPM and RPM?

### Is YouTube CPM the amount a creator earns per 1,000 views?

No. CPM is based on advertiser cost per 1,000 ad impressions, before YouTube's revenue share. RPM estimates creator revenue per 1,000 video views. Since many views do not have an ad impression and the denominators differ, CPM is not a per-view creator payout.

### Why is my YouTube RPM lower than my CPM?

CPM and RPM use different denominators and describe different sides of monetization. RPM is based on creator revenue relative to total views, including views without an ad, while CPM measures advertiser spend per 1,000 ad impressions. Revenue share and the mix of monetization sources also affect the comparison.

### Does YouTube RPM include every source of creator income?

No. The exact revenue included depends on the metric and Analytics report. RPM can combine eligible YouTube revenue sources, but it does not represent every business income stream. Sponsorships, merchandise, and off-platform sales should be tracked separately unless a report explicitly includes them.

### Can I use CPM to predict exactly how much a YouTube video will earn?

No. CPM-based calculations are rough scenarios, not guaranteed payouts. Actual earnings depend on views, monetized playbacks, audience, ad availability, content eligibility, revenue share, and the period being measured. Use YouTube Studio for actual channel results and treat estimates as planning tools.
