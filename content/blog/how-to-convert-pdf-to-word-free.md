To convert a PDF to Word for free, use a browser-based tool that extracts selectable text into a Word-compatible document instead of paying for software or waiting for an emailed result. Convaudit processes the PDF locally in your browser; because it rebuilds text rather than the original page design, formatting can shift.

## Why convert a PDF to Word?

PDFs are convenient for sharing a finished document, but editing their text can be difficult. You may want to update a résumé, reuse text from a report, or correct a paragraph without buying a desktop subscription or creating an account with a service that emails a download link.

A conversion can make text easier to edit, but not every PDF contains text that can be extracted. Scanned pages are images of text and generally need optical character recognition (OCR) before their words can be edited.

## How does a free PDF-to-Word converter work?

Convaudit's [PDF to Text tool](/tools/pdf-to-word) reads selectable text from a PDF in your browser and packages it in a Word-compatible `.doc` file. It does not recreate the original page design as a fully formatted Word document. Lines, columns, images, tables, and other complex layout elements may be changed or omitted.

The conversion runs locally in the browser, so the selected PDF is not sent to Convaudit's server for conversion. You do not need to wait for an email containing a link to the output. The tool is useful when your main goal is to retrieve and edit text, not when you need an exact visual replica.

## How do you convert a PDF to Word for free?

1. Open the [PDF to Word-compatible text tool](/tools/pdf-to-word).
2. Select a PDF that contains selectable text.
3. Start the conversion and download the generated `.doc` file.
4. Open it in Microsoft Word, Google Docs, or another compatible editor.
5. Review the text and formatting, then make any edits needed.

If a document contains sensitive information, follow your organization's handling rules even when processing locally in a browser. Keep the original PDF so you can compare it with the extracted document or retry the conversion if needed.

## What formatting should you expect after conversion?

The tool prioritizes text extraction rather than reproducing the PDF's page layout. Paragraphs may wrap differently, spacing can change, and columns, tables, or images may be altered or left out. A scanned PDF without a selectable text layer may not yield editable text at all. Review the output and correct formatting in your word processor before sharing or submitting it.

For documents where layout fidelity is essential, use a conversion workflow designed to preserve page structure and check the result carefully. For text reuse or basic editing, a text-focused `.doc` may be a simpler starting point.

### Does the converter preserve the PDF's original formatting?

Not reliably. It extracts selectable text and packages it in a Word-compatible `.doc` document, but it does not preserve the page design. Tables, columns, images, and complex layouts may shift or be omitted. Review the downloaded document in your word processor and adjust the layout before relying on it.

### Can I convert a scanned PDF to editable Word text?

Usually not directly with a text extractor. A scan stores each page as an image, so it may not contain selectable characters for the tool to retrieve. You generally need OCR software to recognize the text first. If the PDF lets you select and copy its words, it is more likely to contain extractable text.
