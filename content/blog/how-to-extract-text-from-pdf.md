To extract text from a PDF for free, use a browser-based PDF text extractor to read its selectable text, then copy the result or download it as a `.txt` file. This is useful for quoting or reusing words; converting a PDF to Word is a different task when you want an editable document. The extraction runs locally in your browser.

## What is the difference between extracting text and converting PDF to Word?

Text extraction gives you the words as plain text, which is convenient for search, copy-and-paste, or reuse in another app. Converting to Word produces a document file intended for editing, but it may try to arrange text into paragraphs or other formatting. Neither option reliably recreates a complex PDF layout, and images of text require OCR before the words can be extracted.

## How do you extract text from a PDF in your browser?

1. Open the [PDF to Text tool](/tools/pdf-to-text).
2. Select a PDF that contains selectable text.
3. Start extraction and review the text preview.
4. Copy the text to your clipboard, or download the plain `.txt` file.

The [PDF text extractor](/tools/pdf-to-text) uses your browser to read the PDF and prepare the output; the selected document is not uploaded to Convaudit for processing. The browser may still need an internet connection to load the page. Local processing also means very large or complex documents depend on your device’s available memory.

## Can you extract text from a scanned PDF?

Not unless the scan has a searchable text layer. A scanned page may be stored as an image, so selecting its visible letters does not work and a text extractor cannot read them as characters. Use OCR (optical character recognition) to recognize text in page images, then proofread the results because OCR can misread small, blurry, or unusual type.

## How do you copy or download the extracted text?

After extraction, use **Copy text** to place the preview’s text on your clipboard and paste it into another app. Choose **Download .txt** to save a plain-text copy on your device. A `.txt` file preserves the extracted words and line breaks, not the PDF’s fonts, columns, images, or page design. Review sensitive content before sharing or storing the output.

## What are common questions about extracting text from PDFs?

### Is it free to extract text from a PDF?

Yes. The [PDF to Text tool](/tools/pdf-to-text) is free to use in your browser and does not require desktop software. Select a PDF with selectable text, run extraction, then copy the result or download a `.txt` file. Scanned pages may need OCR first, and complex PDFs can take longer or use more browser memory.

### Does extracting text preserve the original PDF formatting?

No. A plain-text extract contains words and basic line breaks, not the page design. Fonts, columns, tables, images, and precise spacing may be lost or represented differently. If you need a formatted editable document, use a PDF-to-Word workflow and review its layout; if you need the exact appearance, keep the original PDF.

### Can I get text from an image-only scanned PDF?

Usually not with text extraction alone. An image-only scan contains page pictures rather than selectable characters, so the tool has no text layer to copy. Run OCR on the PDF first, then inspect the recognized words for mistakes. Handwriting, low resolution, skewed pages, and faint printing can reduce OCR accuracy.

### Is my PDF uploaded when I extract its text?

No. The [PDF to Text tool](/tools/pdf-to-text) reads the selected PDF locally in your browser, and the file is not sent to Convaudit’s server for processing. This describes the tool’s processing path, not how you should handle the extracted text afterward; follow any privacy rules that apply to your document.
